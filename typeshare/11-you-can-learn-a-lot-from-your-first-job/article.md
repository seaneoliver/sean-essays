# You can learn a lot from your first job

**I was a 19-year-old working with juvenile sex offenders, feces smearing, deviants.**

My first job in college was as a Youth and Family counselor.

I worked with severely behaviorally disturbed kids. In their treatment, under my supervision, we worked on their issues in public.

I learned much about patience and responsibility.

### Patience came from creating conditions for these kids to win.

That meant creating situations where a kid with a pattern of stealing had a choice. Steal or not steal. Make a better choice or stick to old patterns.

### **They made better decisions in their own time.**

The biggest challenge was working incognito. I had to respect client privacy. Average folks would have a problem with me disciplining a client. I learned to deal with a distrustful public. How to manage cops. All in a day's work.

**When your first job starts on expert-level difficulty, everything else is easy.**

Perspective is something we can all get. All you have to do is widen your experience. Do something a little harder.

**Expand your experience.**

It will reset your expectations. That's growth. Hopefully, you won't get bit, spit, or peed on.

**Think of that on your next Zoom call.**
