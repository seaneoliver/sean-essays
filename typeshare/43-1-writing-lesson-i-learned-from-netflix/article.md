# 1 Writing Lesson I Learned From Netflix

**I've watched a LOT of Netflix during Covid.**

I realized the sobering truth after consuming my third straight "British Baking Show" episode. Netflix was great at serving me what I wanted to watch. They constantly worked to keep me watching.

**They had spot-on suggestions.**

Netflix knew me better than I anticipated. Every time I thought I'd watch just one thing, I'd watch much more. What sorcery was this?

**It was data-driven by my behavior and others like me.**

Sure, they made a recommendation, but then they tracked what happened after. That lesson is an important one for any writer or content creator. You may have a hunch on what folks might enjoy starting with.

**The magic happens AFTER you act to:**

- Publish

- Post

- Share

**You need to act first.**

Then you have tangible proof to test that hunch. Only from doing, can you find out what's working. Take all the "what ifs, what abouts, etc" and replace them with actual action. You don't need a crystal ball.

> "F_ck around and find out" - Kids these days

Two choices. Do. Or, do not.

> "I'm trying to free your mind, Neo. But I can only show you the door. You're the one that has to walk through it." - Morpheus

Assuming you do put something out in the universe, that's where learning kicks in. Feedback shows up in the real world.

**None of this happens in your head.**

Sometimes Netflix misses the mark and makes a terrible suggestion. Unfortunately, perfect recommendations don't exist. They still made the recommendation.

**If a business as big as Netflix can survive a crappy recommendation, surely you can share your writing without dying.**

It might be bad. It might be great! *But you can't learn from a mistake you don't make.*
