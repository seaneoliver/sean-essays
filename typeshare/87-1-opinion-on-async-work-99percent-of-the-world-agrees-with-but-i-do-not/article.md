# 1 Opinion On Async Work 99% Of The World Agrees With-But I Do Not

**Remote work has sparked diverse opinions, and over the past five years, my own views have evolved dramatically.**\
\
**Initially, I aligned with the mainstream beliefs with meetings and calls as the default way to get work done.**\
However, as I delved deeper into the remote work culture, I realized many of these accepted norms didn't hold up under scrutiny. The idea that "quick calls" or impromptu meetings are inherently productive is misleading. They often disrupt workflow and don't respect individual time management.

**The crux of efficient remote work lies in asynchronous communication.**\
Writing, contrary to spontaneous calls, demands clarity and precision. It requires the sender to organize their thoughts and questions meticulously, ensuring that every communication is purposeful and concise.

**This approach not only respects the recipient's time by allowing them to respond at their convenience but also enhances overall communication efficiency.**

In practice, replacing "Can we talk?" with a well-structured email or message can prevent unnecessary interruptions and foster a culture of respect and efficiency. It’s crucial to challenge the default impulse to default to synchronous meetings and consider if asynchronous communication could be more effective.

**Meetings and calls have their place, particularly for complex decision-making or sensitive discussions, but they should not be the default.**

By prioritizing written communication, we can respect each other's time and create a more thoughtful, efficient remote working environment.
