# Why You Should Stop Giving Advice

**It's going to save you time and increase your happiness.**

I've been a bit of a know-it-all and loved giving advice. I'd share options, strategies, and suggestions for anyone and everyone who sought my expertise.

**They always fell into two buckets:**

- What should I do about \_\_\_\_?

&nbsp;

- How can I \_\_\_\_\_?

**Now, I have stopped giving all advice for three reasons.**

First, I don't give advice because my assumptions were wrong.

People ask for advice when they lack information, and that lack is the obstacle. Lack of information isn't the limiting factor though, experience is.

**There is no substitute for experience.**

Think of when a friend shared a fun vacation, a hard workout, or a difficulty they had. Is a vicarious experience enough?

**Probably not.**

Second, I don't give advice is because the form is low value

This question focuses on the method, which isn't ideal.

Any answer I give is autobiographical.

There is no universal answer.

**It's only based on your experience.**

Let's assume the person asking for advice is trying to do something that has been done before, something like:

- Get a job

&nbsp;

- Learn Spanish

&nbsp;

- Communicate better with their spouse

**All of this has been done before.**

- There's Google.

&nbsp;

- There's asking about their experience.

&nbsp;

- Go look at how people have already solved it, and try it out, do it.

**The last reason I stopped giving advice is that these questions focus on simulation.**

Anyone asking these questions isn't taking action. Someone who is in motion and actively trying to solve their problem will find a way, regardless of the advice they receive. If it's important enough, they'll find a way.

And if it's not important enough, they will find an excuse.

**Don't give advice.**
