# 3 Science-Backed Reasons For Analog Notes

**Science backs up the significant advantages of written notes over their typed counterparts.**

Here are the three key benefits:

**First, written notes enhance memory encoding.** As you write, you're actively engaging your brain in a way that typing does not. The physical act of writing creates a deeper cognitive connection to the information, which results in better encoding in your memory. This process makes the information more accessible when it's time to recall ([**Mueller & Oppenheimer, 2014**](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0956797614524581)).

**Second, taking written notes fosters a deeper understanding and comprehension of the material.** This comes from the necessity of summarizing and paraphrasing information as you write it down. This active processing of information promotes better understanding and comprehension as you have to distill complex ideas into your own words ([**Bui, Myerson & Hale, 2013**](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3891656/)).

**Finally, written notes improve recall.** Studies show that individuals who take written notes are more successful in recalling information later on. This is because the act of writing engrains the information into your memory more effectively than typing, making it easier for you to remember the details when you need them ([**Longcamp et al., 2008**](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0028393207001750)).

So, don't underestimate the power of the pen. Even in our tech-driven world, the act of writing notes by hand can significantly boost your memory, understanding, and recall, facilitating a deeper and more effective learning experience.
