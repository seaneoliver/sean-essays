# The 3 Things That Separate 2-Month Job Searches from 12-Month Ones

*The ones who land roles fast do three things differently - and it's not what you think*

# Standing Out When Everyone's Getting Cut

Microsoft just quietly eliminated 15,000 people across two major layoffs.

No fanfare. No internal messaging. Just... gone.

I've been here 312 working days, and the constant fear of being "disappeared" is real. It's hard to deliver impact when you're looking over your shoulder.

But here's what I've learned from coaching dozens of people through their job searches during this mess: **The ones who land roles fast do three things differently.**

## Take Care of Your Network Before You Need It

**I can count on one hand the people who could call me with a favor, no questions asked.** Everyone else? They better ask about my weekend plans before diving into their LinkedIn request.

If you can't remember the last time we talked about anything other than work, you're not ready to ask for help. Build that social capital first.

## Get Uncomfortable with Self-Promotion

This is where most people fail. They'll apply to 50 jobs online but won't message their 200 Facebook friends about their situation.

Here's the reality: If you needed a bone marrow match, you'd contact everyone. Treat your career the same way.

**The people who land roles fast use every available channel.** They adapt. They try new approaches. They get specific about what they want and share it widely.

The ones still searching six months later? They're doing comfortable activities. Familiar routines. One-time efforts.

## Maximize Your Surface Area

Get clear on exactly what type of work you want. Down to the specifics. Then represent that clearly to as many people as possible.

**Surface area creates opportunities - both now and later.**

I've watched this pattern play out repeatedly. The difference between a 2-month search and a 12-month search usually comes down to willingness to do uncomfortable things consistently.
