# Why I'm terrified about going back to the office

*The real cost of in-person work isn't what you think.*

After almost five years of remote work, my company pushed hard for return to office. Most people blame lost productivity or weak culture.

The real issue is something more dangerous.

Communication chaos multiplies misalignment faster than you can track it.

Remote work forced information into a few clear channels. Teams meetings with transcripts. Emails you can search. Recorded sessions you can replay.

In-person work brings back hallway chats, shoulder taps, and verbal info dumps that disappear the moment they happen.

You cannot record a live conversation.

Nobody is documenting the chat you had while pouring coffee. Or the thing someone shouted over your shoulder while you were focused on a deadline. Multiply that by every in-person interaction.

Misalignment compounds.

I live in written notes. I write to understand what I know. But some people do not know what they think until they say it.

If I am note-taking Superman, verbal processors are my Lex Luthor.

And at the speed my organization moves, that fills me with dread.

The real problem is not productivity. It is traceability. Remote work created an audit trail.

In-person work erases it. Conversations happen. Decisions get made. Context disappears.

So build systems that catch the unstructured stuff.

- Send a message after a hallway conversation.

- Create shared notes.

- Capture decisions before they drift.

Make alignment easy.

Your team will thank you later.
