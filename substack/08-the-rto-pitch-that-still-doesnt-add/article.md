#  The RTO Pitch That Still Doesn't Add Up

*It's not about productivity - it's about trust*

**I've heard several pitches for return to the office (RTO) and none have convinced me.**

Most start with the claim that the company is at its best when people are in the office 3-5 days a week. This includes companies with offices across the US and often the world.\

**This excludes leadership.**

Here's what strikes me about these pitches.

Pandemic-era remote work gets reframed as a temporary stopgap, not a capability that was built, tested, and strengthened. We spent years proving distributed teams could deliver exceptional results. Now that proof gets erased from the narrative.

One of the more novel pitches? A nostalgic call to some golden era - a promise to recapture the "energy and productivity" of days gone by.

It's the first time I've seen the emotional appeal take the lead. I wonder if this will become the standard bottom-up tactic for selling RTO as the "new normal."

But here's what nobody addresses.

**What's the real difference between taking a Zoom or Teams call from home versus from the office?**

When your team spans Seattle, Austin, and Bangalore, you're still looking at screens. You're still on calls. The conference room doesn't magically make distributed collaboration disappear.

**The data tells a different story than the pitches.**

Microsoft's own Work Trend Index shows 87% of employees report being productive working hybrid or remote. Yet 85% of leaders say the shift to hybrid work makes it challenging to have confidence employees are being productive.

**That's not a productivity problem. That's a trust problem.**

Smart companies are solving this differently. They're focusing on outcomes, not hours in seats. They're building intentional culture through deliberate connection points, not forced proximity. They're recognizing that flexibility has become table stakes for attracting top talent.

The companies winning the talent war aren't the ones mandating returns. **They're the ones who learned from the past four years and evolved.**

You've proven you can deliver from anywhere. You've built the skills to collaborate across time zones and screens. You know that productivity isn't about where you sit - it's about having clear goals, the right tools, and trust from leadership.

The next time you hear an RTO pitch, ask the real questions. What specific problems are we solving? How will this work for our distributed teams? And most importantly - what are we optimizing for?

**Because if it's not trust and results, we're having the wrong conversation.**
