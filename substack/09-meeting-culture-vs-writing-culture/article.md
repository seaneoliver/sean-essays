# Meeting culture vs. writing culture (and who wins)

*The uncomfortable truth about leadership preferences*

I’ve been watching Microsoft leaders for 15 years, and I think I’ve cracked the code on why return-to-office is really happening.

It’s not about collaboration. It’s not about innovation. **It’s about meetings.**

Microsoft is fundamentally a meeting culture. Pull up anyone’s calendar - engineering, marketing, doesn’t matter - and you’ll see half their day blocked out in back-to-back meetings. These are verbal exchanges where personality rules. Where visibility comes from being the loudest voice in the room.

**But here’s the thing: Microsoft is not a writing culture.**

People would rather schedule a “quick call” than spend five minutes crafting a thoughtful email. They’ll pull you into a conference room instead of writing a clear document with recommended next steps. This works when everyone’s in the same building. It breaks down completely when teams are distributed.

Now layer in the expensive campus refresh happening in Redmond right now. Billions spent on shiny new spaces that need to be justified.

The divide is obvious once you see it. Some leaders have built strong async muscles - sharing thoughtful updates, writing clear documentation, keeping distributed teams aligned. Others built their entire careers on being in the room, commanding attention, controlling the dynamics.

Power for some thrives on proximity. And right now, the leaders whose power depends on in-person visibility are winning.

So we’re regressing back to the strengths of incumbent leadership - regardless of what actually makes teams more effective.
