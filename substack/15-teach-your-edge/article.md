# Teach your edge

Your instinct to protect the gap is wrong more often than it is right.

When you notice you’re better than most people around you, the reflex is to stop talking about it. If you teach your methodology, you close the advantage that made you valuable.

But you cannot build a reputation on a secret. You can build one on a framework that other people use and attribute to you.

The gap is temporary regardless. Someone else will figure it out. Write about it. Build a course. The question is whether you get credit for naming the thing.

This is the exact situation with AI collaboration right now. The patterns that produce a 40% quality improvement over typical users are real and teachable. They will also become common knowledge within a few years as best practices spread.

Your choice: be the person who taught your team, your coworkers, your network how to work with AI, or be the person who had an edge for 18 months and blended back into the baseline.

Authority compounds. Secrecy doesn’t.

The exception is narrow. If your advantage is the entire basis of a business with no other moat, teaching it away is genuinely destructive. But for individual practitioners and thought leaders, the math almost always favors making the methodology explicit.

Visibility compounds. Making your thinking public is the first step to both improving it and getting credit for it.

What’s the thing you’re currently protecting that might actually grow faster if you shared it?
